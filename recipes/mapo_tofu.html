Mapo Tofu

Mapo Tofu originated in 1862 in Chengdu, Sichuan, during the Qing Dynasty. It was invented by a pockmarked woman named Mrs. Chen, who ran a small roadside eatery with her husband near the Wanfu Bridge. Catering primarily to laborers, oil porters, and travelers, Mrs. Chen cooked with whatever cheap, accessible ingredients the working class brought her—typically tofu, ground meat, and spices. By combining these staples with fermented broad bean paste (doubanjiang) and Sichuan peppercorns, she created a uniquely spicy and numbing dish.

Ingredients
  • 1 block of firm tofu, cut into cubes
  • 200g ground pork or beef
  • 2 tablespoons doubanjiang (fermented broad bean paste)
  • 1 tablespoon soy sauce
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 teaspoon ginger, minced
  • 1 cup chicken broth
  • 1 teaspoon cornstarch mixed with 2 tablespoons water
  • 2 teaspoons Sichuan peppercorns, toasted and ground
  • 2 green onions, chopped for garnish

Steps
  1. Heat a pan over medium heat and add a bit of oil. Sauté the garlic and ginger until fragrant.
  2. Add the ground meat and cook until browned.
  3. Stir in the doubanjiang, soy sauce, and sugar. Cook for another minute.
  4. Add the chicken broth and bring to a simmer.
  5. Gently add the tofu cubes and let them cook for 5-7 minutes, allowing them to absorb the flavors.
  6. Stir in the cornstarch mixture to thicken the sauce.
  7. Sprinkle with ground Sichuan peppercorns and garnish with chopped green onions before serving.
